**Incidence** refers to the number of new cases of a disease or condition that occur within a specific period in a defined population. It's often expressed as a rate, such as the number of cases per 100,000 people per year.

## Incidence proportion

To calculate incidence proportion (also known as cumulative incidence), follow these steps:

$$
\text{Incidence proportion } = \left(\frac{\text{subjects developing the disease over given period}}{\text{subjects followed over given period}}\right) \times 100
$$

## Incidence rate

But what if our population becomes a little bit more dynamic, i.e. students living in a dorm over, let's say, three years with people moving in and out. This kind of population requires a different type of denominator, namely the **person-time at risk**. For example a student who moved out after 12 months contributes only 12 months to the person-time at risk, while a student who stayed all three years contributes 36 months. So, written as a formula, we have:

$$
\text{Incidence rate} = \left(\frac{\text{subjects developing the disease over given period}}{\text{Total person-time at risk}}\right) \times 100
$$

where total person-time at risk is calculated by summing up the time each individual was at risk. For instance:

$$
\text{Total person-time at risk} = \newline (\text{Student A's duration at risk}) + (\text{Student B's duration at risk}) + \ldots
$$

## What's the difference between incidence and prevalence?

While incidence focuses on new cases, [[prevalence]] refers to the total number of cases (both new and old) at a given point in time. In summary, incidence is about new cases over time, while prevalence is about total cases at a point in time.
