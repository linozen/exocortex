> The noun was coined by the American ecological psychologist James J. Gibson. An affordance is an available use or purpose of a thing or an entity.

— [Concilience Project glossary](https://consilienceproject.org/glossary/), citing Gibson.

A chair affords sitting. A handle affords pulling. A doorway affords passage. The point of Gibson's concept: the affordance is _relational_ — it depends on both the object's properties _and_ the agent's capabilities. A staircase affords climbing for one person and an obstacle for another.

## Why it matters beyond UX

The concept escaped its psychology-of-perception origins and now does work in:

- **Design** — Norman's _The Design of Everyday Things_ applied affordances to product design; the term became foundational to UX.
- **Technology critique** — the affordances of a tool partly determine what behaviours it makes easy. A swipe-based dating app affords disposable evaluation; a long-form letter affords considered attention. See [[technologien-der-intimität]] for affordance-thinking applied to intimacy tech.
- **Architecture** — Christopher Alexander's pattern language operationalizes affordances at the scale of buildings and cities.
- **Sense-making** — affordances of a media environment shape what kinds of [[sensemaking]] are easy or hard.
