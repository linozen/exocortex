Caffeine withdrawal is a clinically recognized syndrome that emerges when a chronic user reduces or stops intake. It was added as a formal diagnosis in **DSM-5 (2013)** — code **F15.93** in ICD-10 / 292.0 in DSM coding — on the strength of a critical review by [@juliano2004] that synthesized 57 experimental studies and 9 survey studies.

## Neurobiology

The core mechanism is the **rebound** of [[adenosine]] signaling after chronic antagonism by [[caffeine]]:

1. Chronic A1 and A2A receptor antagonism by caffeine leads to **upregulation** of those receptors in cortex, basal ganglia, and brainstem arousal centers.
2. When caffeine clears, endogenous adenosine acts on a *sensitized* receptor field, producing exaggerated inhibitory signaling.
3. Downstream consequences:
   - **Cerebral vasodilation** → throbbing headache (the most reliable withdrawal sign).
   - **Reduced dopaminergic tone in prefrontal cortex** → dysphoria, low motivation, slowed cognition [@rochacabrero2025].
   - **Reduced noradrenergic activation** → fatigue, drowsiness.
   - Chronic caffeine also alters densities of adrenergic, cholinergic, GABA, and serotonin receptors; their re-equilibration contributes to the multi- day symptom course.

## DSM-5 diagnostic criteria

Three or more of the following symptoms, developing within **24 hours** of abrupt cessation or reduction after prolonged daily use, causing clinically significant distress or functional impairment, and not attributable to another disorder:

1. Headache
2. Marked fatigue or drowsiness
3. Dysphoric mood, depressed mood, or irritability
4. Difficulty concentrating
5. Flu-like symptoms (nausea, vomiting, muscle pain/stiffness)

[@juliano2004; @rochacabrero2025]

## Epidemiology

- Withdrawal can be precipitated by abstaining from doses as low as **100 mg/day** (about one mug of coffee) [@juliano2004].
- Headache is the single most common sign, present in ~50% of controlled abstinence trials [@juliano2004].
- Roughly **13%** of caffeine users experience clinically significant functional impairment from withdrawal [@rochacabrero2025].

## Management

- **Gradual taper** over 1–2 weeks (e.g., reduce daily intake by ~25% every few days) substantially attenuates symptoms.
- Hydration, rest, OTC analgesics for headache.
- A small "rescue" dose (50–100 mg caffeine) gives near-immediate relief if withdrawal becomes disabling — useful diagnostically as well [@rochacabrero2025].

## Open questions / things to verify

- How much of withdrawal is **expectancy / nocebo**? Juliano's 2019 balanced- placebo study suggests an expectancy component but does not eliminate the pharmacological signal.
- Individual variability is large; CYP1A2 polymorphisms modulate both half-life and reported withdrawal severity.
- The dopamine PFC story is more secure in animal models than in humans.
