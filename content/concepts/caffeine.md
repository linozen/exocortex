Caffeine (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine) is the most widely consumed psychoactive substance in the world. Roughly **80–90% of North American adults** use it regularly, with a mean U.S. intake around **280 mg/day** (~1–2 cups of brewed coffee) [@rochacabrero2025].

## Mechanism of action

See [[pharmacodynamics]] for the general receptor-binding framework this applies. Caffeine's stimulant effect is *not* a direct excitation but a **disinhibition**: it competitively blocks [[adenosine]] receptors, primarily A1 and A2A. With adenosine prevented from signaling fatigue, downstream consequences include:

1. **Increased arousal** — A2A blockade in the basal ganglia / VLPO removes the sleep-promoting drive.
2. **Tonic dopamine release** — A2A receptors form heteromers with dopamine D2 receptors in the striatum; blocking A2A potentiates dopaminergic transmission, contributing to the reinforcing/mood-elevating effect [@rochacabrero2025].
3. **Noradrenergic activation** — secondary increase in norepinephrine drives peripheral effects (heart rate, vasoconstriction).
4. **Cerebral vasoconstriction** — adenosine is a cerebrovascular dilator; blocking it constricts cerebral vessels, which is the proximate cause of caffeine's headache-relieving effect and, conversely, the **rebound vasodilation headache** seen in [[caffeine-withdrawal]].

## Pharmacokinetics

See [[pharmacokinetics]] for the general ADME framework.

- Oral bioavailability ~100%; peak plasma 30–60 minutes after ingestion.
- Plasma half-life typically 3–5 hours in healthy adults, but highly variable with CYP1A2 genotype, pregnancy, smoking, and oral contraceptive use.
- Up to **400 mg/day** is generally considered safe for non-pregnant adults [@rochacabrero2025].

## Tolerance

Chronic intake upregulates [[adenosine]] A1 and A2A receptors and alters densities of adrenergic, cholinergic, GABA, and serotonin receptors and calcium channels in the brain. This receptor remodeling is the substrate for both **tolerance** (diminished subjective stimulant effect at the same dose) and **withdrawal** (symptomatic state when the antagonist is removed).
