Developmental psychologist **Edward Tronick** (best known for the [Still-Face Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_face_experiment)) reframes consciousness as something a dyad does together, rather than something each mind has separately. Meaning isn't transmitted across the gap between two people — it's _made_ in the gap, by both at once.

## Tronick's reframe

> "Das gemeinsame, ko-kreative Schaffen eines neuen Sinns stellt die Beziehung her. Es ist nicht die Beziehung, die den Sinn erzeugt. Es mag notwendig sein 'mit dem anderen zu sein', 'empathisch zu sein', 'präsent zu sein', all das ist aber nicht ausreichend, um eine Beziehung aufzubauen oder eine therapeutische Veränderung einzuleiten. Ein aktiver gemeinsamer diadischer Bewußtseinszustand, das Erzeugen eines neuen Sinns, initiiert den Veränderungsprozess, den Prozess des Beziehungsaufbaus und erlaubt es, den anderen kennenzulernen."

— Tronick, _Multilevel psychobiological meaning making and empathy_ ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mGqgGM1AkPw))

The inversion that matters: presence, empathy, and being-with are necessary but not sufficient. What _creates_ the relationship — and what initiates therapeutic change — is the **active joint making of new meaning**. The relationship is a byproduct of co-creation, not its substrate.

## Still-Face Experiment

Tronick's 1975 experiment: a parent interacts normally with their infant for two minutes, then suddenly adopts a still, expressionless face for two minutes. The infant's distress response — attempts to re-engage, then withdrawal, then disorganization — is rapid and reliable. The result demonstrates that infants don't just need a present caregiver; they need a _responsive_ one, because regulation happens in the loop, not inside the infant.
