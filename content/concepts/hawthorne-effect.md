The Hawthorne effect names the tendency of subjects to modify their behaviour when they are aware of being studied — independently of the variable the study is manipulating. Named after the Hawthorne Works (Western Electric) studies of the 1920s–30s, where productivity improved under both increased and decreased lighting, suggesting it was attention itself driving the change.

## Implications

- Any behavioural intervention with measurable outcomes has a confound: the act of measuring is itself a treatment.
- Industry/UX research that depends on opt-in observation overestimates the effect of whatever it tests.
- Even self-tracking (steps, sleep, journaling) plausibly biases the tracked behaviour upward.
