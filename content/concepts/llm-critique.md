Stub anchor for the strand of writing that pushes back on the "LLMs change everything" narrative. Not anti-AI for its own sake — but the cleaner the critique, the more useful the technology becomes once you know its actual edge.

## Starting bibliography

- ["Just a Statistical Text Predictor" - asanai.net](http://www.asanai.net/2023/05/14/just-a-statistical-text-predictor/) — defense of the dismissive framing, taken seriously.
- [Gary Marcus — Elegant and powerful new result that seriously undermines large language models](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/elegant-and-powerful-new-result-that)
- [Bruce Schneier — AI and mass spying](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2023/12/ai-and-mass-spying.html) — what changes when the cost of reading everyone's mail drops to zero.
- [Ars Technica — Time saved by AI offset by new work created (study)](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2025/05/time-saved-by-ai-offset-by-new-work-created-study-suggests/) — empirical pushback on productivity claims.
- [UnHerd — Why I'm boycotting AI](https://unherd.com/2025/03/why-im-boycotting-ai/)
- [ia.net — Turning the tables on AI](https://ia.net/topics/turning-the-tables-on-ai)
- [Pillar Security — New vulnerability in GitHub Copilot and Cursor](https://www.pillar.security/blog/new-vulnerability-in-github-copilot-and-cursor-how-hackers-can-weaponize-code-agents) — agent weaponization.
