> A process by which people interpret information and experiences, and structure their understanding of a given domain of knowledge.

— [Concilience Project glossary](https://consilienceproject.org/glossary/)

Sensemaking is the activity, not the product. It's what people _do_ to turn ambiguity into action — at the individual level (figuring out what's going on around me), at the group level (figuring out what we collectively believe is going on), and at the civilizational level (figuring out what kind of moment we're in).

A working sensemaking infrastructure is what lets a society respond coherently to novelty. When it breaks down — as the Concilience Project argues it has — every other coordination problem becomes harder.

## Why it matters now

The thesis Schmachtenberger and colleagues push: we face civilizational-scale problems (ecological, technological, political) whose complexity exceeds the sense-making capacity of our current epistemic infrastructure. Social media optimizes for engagement, not for shared understanding. Algorithmic amplification of outrage destroys the [[epistemic-commons]]. Without functioning collective sense-making, we cannot collectively respond to anything that requires coordination at scale.
