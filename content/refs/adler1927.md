Alfred Adler founded **Individual Psychology** as a counter to Freudian drive theory and Jungian archetype theory. Adler's frame: human behaviour is **forward-looking** — oriented toward what we're striving _toward_, not driven by what happened to us. The future shapes the present more than the past does.

## Core concepts

### Self-ideal

The ideal version of the person we aspire to become. Forms in childhood through engagement with the social environment. Once fixed, it shapes goals, motivations, and the _lifestyle_ we develop to navigate challenges. Critically: when the self-ideal misaligns with reality, neurosis follows.

### Striving for superiority

A natural drive to improve one's circumstances and achieve a more advantageous position in life. Not "superiority over others" — superiority over one's prior self, in the direction of the self-ideal. Pathology arises when this striving turns competitive rather than developmental.

### Lifestyle and inferiority feelings

_Lifestyle_ here means the unspoken decision-rules a person uses to make choices. _Inferiority feelings_ — whether grounded in real shortcomings or perceived ones — can either propel growth or block it, depending on how the lifestyle handles them.

### Coping vs safeguarding

Two response patterns to inferiority feelings:

- **Coping** — directly addressing challenges (improving skills) or compensating for weaknesses (excelling elsewhere).
- **Safeguarding** — excuses, avoidance, self-sabotage (procrastination, anxiety, somatization) to escape discomfort. Sustained safeguarding solidifies into an **inferiority complex**.

The distinction matters clinically: safeguarding behaviours look like symptoms but are actually _strategies_ — the question is what they protect against.

### Courage

For Adler, the central work of psychotherapy is the cultivation of **courage** — the willingness to embrace uncertainty and take risks for growth. Courage is what enables a person to confront challenges, adjust unrealistic self-ideals, and replace safeguarding with coping.

## Application notes

- Reflect on the self-ideal: does it align with stated values, or is it a defensive construction?
- Identify recurring safeguarding patterns. For each, ask: _what does this protect me from feeling?_
- Replace one safeguarding strategy at a time with a proactive coping move.
- The aim isn't to eliminate fear — it's to act _despite_ fear, which is the operational definition of courage.

- _The Courage to Be Happy_ — same authors, follow-up.
- Henri Ellenberger, _The Discovery of the Unconscious_ — Adler chapter places him in historical context.
