## Foundational concepts

- **Consilience** — In science and history, the principle that evidence from independent, unrelated sources can "converge" on strong conclusions.
- **Sensemaking** → [[sensemaking]] — A process by which people interpret information and experiences, and structure their understanding of a given domain of knowledge.
- **Affordance** → [[affordance]] — Coined by ecological psychologist James J. Gibson. An available use or purpose of a thing or entity.
- **Hyperobjects** → [[hyperobjects]] — Processes that occupy vast expanses of time and space, defying the traditional sense of an "object" as something that can be singled out.

## Epistemology

- **Epistemology** — The philosophical study of knowing and the nature of knowledge.
- **Empiricism** — A philosophical theory that knowledge is derived from sensory experiences and relies heavily on scientific evidence.
- **Epistemic commons** → [[epistemic-commons]] — Public and private spaces where people come together to pursue mutual understanding of issues critical to their society.
- **Epistemic hubris** — Inadvertent emotionally or politically-motivated closed-mindedness, manifesting as certainty or overconfidence when dealing with complex indeterminate problems.
- **Epistemic humility** — An ethos of learning balanced between confidence and openness to new ideas.
- **Epistemic nihilism** — A diffuse, usually subconscious feeling that it is impossible to really know anything.
- **Paradigm** — The set of concepts and practices that define a scientific discipline at a particular period.

## Cognitive biases / discourse failure modes

- **Confirmation bias** — Selectively seeking, recalling, or favourably interpreting evidence that supports one's current beliefs. See [[cognitive-illusions]].
- **Cherry picking** — Selectively presenting evidence that supports a position while omitting contradicting evidence.
- **Strawman arguments** — Presenting opposing positions in their weakest form, then dismissing them as if the position itself were discredited.
- **Thought-terminating clichés** — Brief, highly-reductive, definitive-sounding phrases that stop further questioning. A propaganda primitive.
- **Frame control** — The ability of an individual or group to shape perception of a topic by setting the narrative and determining the debate's context.
- **Limbic hijack** — Having one's attention captured by emotionally triggering stimuli. See [[attention]].
- **Sensationalism** — Mass-media tactic of choosing and wording stories to maximize emotional reaction and audience.
- **Villainization** — Crafting an anti-hero image of a disagreeable group, contrasted with the best qualities of one's own.

## Information warfare / manipulation

- **Information warfare** — Fourth- and fifth-generation warfare conducted through bits and memes rather than guns and bombs.
- **Non-kinetic warfare** — Rivalry between nation-states or groups conducted by means other than direct physical violence.
- **Algorithmic radicalization** → [[algorithmic-radicalization]] — Ideological polarization generated as a side-effect of recommendation algorithms optimizing for engagement and ad revenue.
- **Microtargeting** — Online advertising that builds personal profiles from individual users' trace data, then targets ads to those profiles. See [[turnkey-tyranny]], [[feldstein2019]].
- **Deep fakes** — Digitally-altered (via AI) recordings of a person for propaganda, sexual objectification, defamation, or parody.
- **Sock puppet** — A fake online persona crafted to manipulate public opinion without implicating the creator.
- **Agent provocateur** — One who persuades another into a crime or rash act, or implicates them in such acts.
- **Misinformation** — False or misleading information, irrespective of intent.
- **Plausible deniability** — Ability of an entity to deny knowing about unethical or illegal activity because there is no evidence to the contrary.
- **Unverifiable** — A proposition or state of affairs that cannot be proven true.

## Political / social structure

- **Public sphere** — Habermas: the collective common spaces where people come together to publicly articulate matters of mutual interest.
- **Open society / Closed society** — Open societies promote free exchange of information and democratic governance based on popular participation; closed societies restrict information exchange and impose top-down decisions.
- **Social contract theory** — Individuals sacrifice some freedom and agree to state authority under specified legal rules.
- **Civic virtue** — Ethical behaviour exhibited by individuals in service of bettering their communities and state, sometimes foregoing personal gain.
- **Commons** — Collective resources in which every participant of the collective has an equal interest.
- **Pedagogy** — The theory and practice of teaching and learning, and how that process influences and is influenced by social, political, and psychological development.
- **Intergenerational transmission** — The foundational process of education underlying societal and cultural cohesion across generations.
- **Cultural enlightenment** — A period when a culture undergoes increasing reflectivity on itself.
- **Renaissance** — French for "rebirth"; refers to periods of great social change.

## Civilizational systems

- **Societal autopoiesis** — The process by which a society maintains its capacity to perpetuate and adapt while preserving relative continuity.
- **Risk society** — Term proposed by sociologists (Beck) for the emergent properties of social systems after WWII — increasingly preoccupied with securing the future against widespread, unpredictable risks. See [[catastrophic-risk]].
- **Technocapitalism** — A worldview holding that technology, specifically as developed by private corporations, is the primary driver of civilizational progress.
- **Monetarism** — A school of economic thought emphasizing the government's role in controlling the supply of money in an economy.

## Good-faith discourse

- **Good faith communications** — Discourse oriented toward mutual understanding and coordinated action, with the result of increasing the faith participants have in the value of communicating.
- **Ethos** — From the Greek for "custom, habit, character"; the set of ideals or customs around which a group coheres.
