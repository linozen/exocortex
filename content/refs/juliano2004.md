## Highlights

- **Authors:** Laura M. Juliano (American University) & Roland R. Griffiths (Johns Hopkins).
- **Venue:** *Psychopharmacology*, Vol. 176, Issue 1, pp. 1–29 (October 2004).
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s00213-004-2000-x
- Reviewed **57 experimental studies + 9 survey studies** on human caffeine withdrawal.
- Identified **five symptom clusters**: (1) headache, (2) fatigue/drowsiness, (3) dysphoric mood including depression and irritability, (4) difficulty concentrating, (5) flu-like symptoms.
- Established the **timeline** used in clinical guidance: onset 12–24 h, peak 20–51 h, duration 2–9 days.
- Demonstrated withdrawal at doses as low as **100 mg/day**.
- Headache was the most frequent withdrawal sign — present in ~50% of controlled abstinence trials.
- Conclusion: evidence sufficient to warrant inclusion of caffeine withdrawal in DSM and revision of ICD criteria. This recommendation was adopted in **DSM-5 (2013)**.

## Why it matters

This is the load-bearing citation behind the formal recognition of [[caffeine-withdrawal]] as a diagnosable condition. Any clinical or popular account of caffeine withdrawal traces, directly or indirectly, to this review.
