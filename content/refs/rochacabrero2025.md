- **Authors:** Franklyn Rocha Cabrero, Richard J. Hamilton.
- **Publisher:** StatPearls Publishing (NCBI Bookshelf, NBK430790).
- **Last updated:** 13 December 2025.
- **URL:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK430790/

### Epidemiology

- 80–90% of North American adults consume caffeine regularly.
- Mean U.S. daily intake ≈ 280 mg (~1–2 cups of brewed coffee).
- Up to 400 mg/day is considered safe for non-pregnant adults.
- ~13% of users experience clinically significant distress from withdrawal.

### Pathophysiology (verbatim summary)

- Caffeine is a competitive antagonist at CNS [[adenosine]] receptors.
- Chronic consumption upregulates adenosine receptors, heightening sensitivity to endogenous adenosine.
- Cessation → enhanced adenosine signaling → fatigue and headache via cerebral vasodilation.
- Reduced dopamine release in prefrontal cortex contributes to mood disturbance and cognitive slowing.

### Symptom timeline

- Onset: 12–24 h after last dose.
- Peak: 20–51 h.
- Total duration: 2–9 days.

### Management

- Gradual taper over several days.
- Hydration, rest, OTC analgesics.
- Small "rescue" dose of 50–100 mg caffeine for disabling symptoms.
